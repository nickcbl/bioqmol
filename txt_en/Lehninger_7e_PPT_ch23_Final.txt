Slide 4:
Neuronal versus Hormonal Signaling
In neuronal signaling, nerve cells release neurotransmitters that act on nearby cells.	 Distance may be small (<1 μm).
In hormonal signaling, hormones are carried by the bloodstream to nearby cells or other organs. Distance may be great (1 m or more).

Slide 5:
Challenges in Detection and Quantification of Hormones
Produced in small amounts, so it is difficult to purify in appreciable quantity.
Chemical analysis of thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) from pigs required one million hypothalmuses (1,000,000 pigs).
The radioimmunoassay (RIA) was developed to be a more sensitive way to measure hormones using radiolabeled antibodies.

Slide 6:
Methods in Detection and Quantification of Hormones
Purified hormone is injected into an animal.
The animal makes an antibody to the hormone.
The antibody is purified and labeled with radioactive tag (for RIA) or an enzyme that produces a colored product (for ELISA).
For a quantitative assay, a known amount of tagged antibody is added to a sample.
The fraction of the antibody bound is measured via photometry (for ELISA) or radiation detection (for RIA).


Slide 7:
Hormone-Receptor Interactions Are Specific and High-Affinity
Different types of cells have different sets of receptors.
Different cells with the same receptor can have different downstream effects.
Even structurally similar hormones can bind different receptors.
Interactions are high-affinity so that only low amounts of hormone are needed.

Slide 29:
The Hypothalamus Is the Coordination Center of the Endocrine System
The hypothalamus is a small region of the forebrain in animals with skulls.
It receives and integrates nerve signals from the central nervous system.
It synthesizes the small peptide hormones oxytocin and vasopressin.
It synthesizes several factors that regulate the function of the anterior pituitary.

Slide 30:

The anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) is the endocrine organ that receives releasing factors from the hypothalamus via blood vessels.
produces long peptide hormones called tropins
activates second targets: adrenal cortex, thyroid, ovaries/testes

Slide 37:
Carbohydrate Sensing and Processing in the Liver
Glucose, fructose, galactose, and mannose convert to glucose-6-phosphate (G6p).
G6p is the “transfer station” in the liver.
can be made by multiple pathways
can have multiple metabolic fates
Hepatocytes have GLUT2 transporter.
allows passive diffusion of glucose in and out
Hepatocytes have glucokinase (hexokinase IV).
has higher Km than other hexokinases
10 mM versus 4 mM 
So g6p isn’t made when glucose low (other tissues need it).
not inhibited by g6p
So g6p can be made continually.

Slide 84:
Diabetes Symptoms
In both forms of diabetes, blood sugar becomes elevated.
The body tries to dilute the glucose, leading to excessive urination and thirst.
In type 1, fat breakdown is accelerated, which leads to high production of ketone bodies.
Some of the ketones are ketoacids, which raise blood [H+], leading to ketoacidosis.
The bicarbonate buffering system is activated, leading to altered breathing.
The breakdown of ketone body acetoacetate produces acetone, which is expelled via the breath.
Untreated diabetes leads to dramatic weight loss.

Slide 85:
Physiological Effects of Blood Glucose Levels
Blood glucose is normally determined after several hours of fasting.
A high fasting blood glucose level (126 or higher) is a warning sign for diabetes.
A low fasting blood glucose level below 50 (in men) or 40 are warning signs of various hypoglycemic conditions.
Blood glucose levels after a meal (postprandial) are typically higher (up to 145 mg/100 mL is normal).

Slide 86:
Long-Term Effects of Elevated Blood Sugar
Proteins can be glycosylated, especially at free amino groups.
Hemoglobin is abundant, has many exposed amino groups during formation, and entry of glucose into erythrocytes is not regulated.
hence, Hb easily glycosylated
compromises O2 delivery, especially in extremities (feet, etc.)
It increases the risk of cardiovascular disease, renal failure, and damage to small blood vessels and nerves.

Restoring leptin to ob/ob mice results in restoration to normal body mass.

Something downstream in leptin response must be defective (“leptin resistance”).

Slide 99:
Adiponectin Is Made by Adipose Tissue and Has Receptors in the Brain
It circulates and makes other organs sensitive to insulin.
While incompletely understood, it appears to work via AMP-activated kinase pathway.
AMPK phosphorylates and inactivates acetyl-CoA carboxylase.
Enzyme normally makes malonyl-CoA.
Malonyl-CoA inhibits fatty acid import into mitochondria.
Reduced acetyl-CoA carboxylase means that fatty acids are free to enter the mitochondria for oxidation.
AMPK pathway also inhibits cholesterol synthesis.

Some microorganisms create fermentation products that affect adipose tissue.

Slide 115:
The “Lipid Burden” Hypothesis to Explain the Path from Obesity to Type 2 Diabetes
80% of type 2 diabetics are obese, but the minority of obese individuals develop diabetes.
Adipocytes become packed and unable to accommodate more TAG.
The inability to deposit TAG leads to FA in the blood.
Excess FA enter muscle and the liver, create TAG lipid droplets, and cause these organs to lose sensitivity to insulin.
	→Ultimately, blood glucose levels rise.
